AgentTerm 0.13.0 speaks protocol version 36: both the Mac and the iPhone have to be 0.13, and so does the host. After updating the Mac app, restart the host when the app offers to, then update the iPhone app.

## New

- **Read is the same on every device.** Read on the Mac, it is read on the iPhone, and the other way round; tapping a notification on the iPhone opens its session and reads it. What was read while a device was away is told when it connects again.
- **Notifications come at the same moment on the Mac and the iPhone, after a short wait.** An agent that is done is told after 30 seconds; one that asks something or needs your permission, after 30 seconds while you are at the Mac and at once while you are not. Answered, opened or read within those 30 seconds, it is shown nowhere. The Mac shows macOS notifications with its app in front too.
- **One Live Activity, kept up to date**, rather than a new one each turn: with nothing working it says so, and it ends after 15 minutes with nothing.
- **Notification permission is asked up front**: in the Permissions step of the Mac's onboarding, and on the iPhone's first screen.
- **A Shells tab splits in three** (Mac): a shell on the right and one below the one picked, turned on from the end of its row; a shell is put in a part from its menu (Show on the Left, on the Right, Below) or dragged there. The part with the keyboard is framed, and the parts are parted by a gutter that is dragged; an agent's area looks the same.
- **Each item of the row shows its shortcut**, ⌥⌘1 to ⌥⌘9, as the strip's tabs show ⌘1 to ⌘9 (Mac).
- **A Mac showing another Mac's page uses its mouse there**: hovering, double clicks, dragging, selecting text and ⌘C to copy it; a right click opens this Mac's own menu (Copy, Open Link in New Tab, Copy Link Address, Back, Forward, Reload).
- **The Inbox shows all of what an agent said**, not its end only, and an item opens whole on both apps, with Open for its session.

## Changed

- The question of what may be sent to the developer is the onboarding's Reports step on the Mac, and the first part of the iPhone's first screen: never two windows over each other at the first launch.
- The Inbox has no Next button.
- Opening a tab updates only what shows it: opening a document holds the Mac's window up about half as long as before, and a Markdown preview after a text file no longer starts a web process of its own.

## Fixed

- With the window closed and the app in the menu bar, opening the app again from the Dock or the Finder showed no window.
- A click in an agent's own part, beside its page or shell, did not move the keyboard there.
- The keyboard was sometimes lost when a file or a diff was picked by its number.
- The onboarding and the reporting question were shown one over the other.
- Showing a shell on the right or below from the left could put one shell in two parts; the left's only shell now stays, and the app says why. A part waiting for its new shell no longer takes one opened elsewhere.
- With the iPhone locked, a notification could reach no device: the iPhone now tells the Mac when it goes to the background, and is told through Apple.
- The Live Activity could stay on the Lock Screen showing done, or not come back once it had ended.
- A failure waiting to be notified was lost if the host restarted meanwhile.
- Not Now on the iPhone's first screen still made iOS ask; it now turns notifications off.
- Switches showed on while what they stand for could not work (notifications while the app is closed, Live Activities turned off in iOS, Privacy in a build with no reporting service); they now show off and say why.
- On a Mac showing another Mac's page: ⌘C right after selecting, keys typed right after a click, and scrolling could reach the page out of order; the page no longer jumps when the figures appear.
- A page open on the iPhone across a reconnect is watched again, and what waited when the connection closed is answered on both apps.
- Asking for a new session while not connected says why nothing started; a program on the app's port that is not a host is named, and never offered to be stopped.

## Known

- Opening a document's tab still holds the Mac's window up 20 to 30 ms, more than a frame (#178).
